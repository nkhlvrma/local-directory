# Branding & Launch Strategy — Index

This folder is exploratory strategy and content work for the hyperlocal, WhatsApp-first local guide currently called "Local Directory" in the codebase (repo: `/Users/ann/Desktop/local-directory`). **Nothing in here is implemented or approved** — no application code, config, or existing docs were touched to produce it. It exists for a human (the founder) to review, edit, argue with, and decide on before anything gets built.

The core promise is deliberately narrow: **find locals you can reach**. The product checks contactability; it does not guarantee service quality, safety, price, availability, or business legitimacy.

## Top naming recommendation

**Karib**, with **Sang** and **Milke** as backups.

The reasoning in short: almost every obviously-fitting Hindi-Urdu word for "nearby" or "neighborhood" — Aaspaas, Nukkad, Padosi, Mohalla, Nazdeek, Chauraha — turned out to already be in active use by a real Indian (or regional) company, several of them funded and in the same or an adjacent category (Nukkad Shops is a VC-backed hyperlocal retail startup; Nazdeek is a live, concept-identical local-services app in Pakistan; Mohalla Tech is the multi-billion-dollar parent of ShareChat/Moj). Karib was the one shortlisted candidate with no material collision found in the web-search spot-check, it means "close/near" — encoding the reachability promise directly rather than needing to be explained — it's short and fast to type one-handed on WhatsApp, it has no ambiguous spelling, and it's the only candidate where the phrase test ("Ask Karib," "Chat with a local through Karib") actually reinforces the brand meaning rather than just sounding neutral. The honest trade-off: it's a less instantly evocative word than Nukkad would have been if that name weren't taken — the tagline and supporting copy need to do more of the explaining. This is a web-search-level check, not trademark clearance — see the "next steps" section at the end of `01-naming.md` before locking anything in.

## Things a human reviewer should decide on or push back on

Being genuinely critical here, as asked, rather than just summarizing the documents:

1. **The naming landscape is more contested than the source plan implies, and this may be worth more than a web-search check.** Every single "obvious" name candidate collided with a real, sometimes funded competitor. That's a signal the hyperlocal-directory space in India is more crowded than the source plan's framing suggests — worth a harder look at direct competitors (Nukkad Shops, Chauraha, similar local WhatsApp-directory plays) before finalizing positioning, not just naming. If Karib is chosen, get real trademark clearance (IP India search) before any public launch — a web search is not that, and I said so explicitly in `01-naming.md`.

2. **The 90-day plan assumes a level of sustained solo-founder execution that's worth stress-testing honestly.** The acquisition playbooks and content calendar in this folder are written to be realistic (in-person visits, connector recruitment, 3–5 hrs/week of content work on top of listing recruitment), but even at that reduced scope, running Phase 1's in-person outreach *and* Phase 2's community/content cadence *and* the eventual dashboard-driven diagnosis work in Phase 3, alone, for 90 straight days, is a lot for one person with no team. Before committing to this calendar, the founder should honestly gut-check how many hours/week are actually available — if it's meaningfully less than what's assumed here, cut Instagram first (per the cadence note in `08-90-day-content-calendar.md`) and consider stretching the 90-day window rather than compressing the work.

3. **The "Would recommend" floor of N=5 (in `06-verification-community-copy.md`) is a considered guess, not a data-backed number — treat it as provisional.** It's reasoned from first principles (low enough to be achievable early, high enough not to look empty), but I have no actual usage data to validate it against. Revisit it explicitly after the first 30 days of real recommendation activity rather than treating 5 as settled.

4. **The second-city checklist may be the most important document to actually enforce, precisely because it's the easiest to skip.** There's a natural pull to treat "one second-city pilot later" as a calendar commitment rather than an earned milestone — `12-second-city-checklist.md` is written specifically to resist that, including a "Gate 0" check on whether Lucknow is even stable enough to share attention. If the 90 days go reasonably well but not spectacularly, there will likely be real pressure to expand anyway on schedule — worth deciding now, while there's no sunk-cost pressure, that the gates in that document are real gates and not just a formality.

5. **Nothing in this folder has been shown to a real Lucknow resident or business owner.** Every voice example, every WhatsApp template, every objection-handling script is a hypothesis. Before public launch, test the name and the trust language with real people; specifically check whether residents understand “Contact checked” and whether businesses understand consent, removal, and future changes.
