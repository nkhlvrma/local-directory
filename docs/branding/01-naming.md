# Naming — Candidates, Research, and Recommendation

**Status:** Exploratory. Nothing here is approved. This document exists so a human can make a naming decision with real information in front of them, not gut feel alone.

**A note on rigor:** the availability checks below are web-search spot checks — I searched for each name plus "app"/"startup", and searched for the literal domain strings. This tells us whether an *obvious, prominent* collision exists and gives a rough read on whether a domain looks parked/available. It is **not** trademark clearance, not a WHOIS lookup, not an app-store name-availability check, and not a check against India's trademark registry (IP India). Before any name is locked in, get an actual trademark search done (or at minimum run each finalist through IP India's public search) and confirm domain/handle availability directly through a registrar and each social platform.

## 2. Shortlist: 8–10 candidates, screened for fit

Screening criteria: **pronunciation** (say it out loud — does it trip on a WhatsApp voice note or a spoken recommendation?), **memorability**, **city-expansion potential** (Lucknow-specific vs. generalizes), **WhatsApp-friendliness** (short, one-thumb typing, no odd spellings).

| Name | Pronunciation | Memorability | Expands beyond Lucknow? | WhatsApp-friendly (length/typing) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aaspaas** | Easy, familiar rhythm (AAS-paas) | High — common word, sticks | Yes, generic Hindi-Urdu, works in any Indian city | 7 letters, double-letter start is mildly fiddly on a phone keyboard but auto-complete handles it |
| **Karib** | Easy, one clean beat | Medium-high — less common as a spoken word than Aaspaas, but clean once heard | Yes, generic | 5 letters, very fast to type |
| **Nukkad** | Easy, iconic | High — vivid, visual word (everyone pictures a street corner) | Yes, but strongly Hindi-belt in flavor — reads a little "Delhi/UP" more than pan-India (fine for Lucknow-first, worth flagging for later cities) | 6 letters |
| **Padosi** | Easy | High — universally understood | Yes, generic | 6 letters |
| **Mohalla** | Easy but longer | High — evocative, warm | Yes, but very strongly "old city / mohalla culture" — could feel more residential-community than business-directory | 7 letters |
| **Nazdeek** | Slightly harder — the "ee" ending isn't always typed correctly (Nazdeek vs Nazdik vs Nazdeeq — three spellings circulate) | Medium | Yes, generic | 7 letters, spelling ambiguity is a real WhatsApp-typing problem |
| **Sang** | Very easy, one syllable | Medium — short but a little thin/abstract on its own; doesn't obviously say "local" without context | Yes, generic, no city flavor at all | 4 letters, fastest to type on the list |
| **Milke** | Easy, friendly imperative feel ("let's meet") | Medium — pleasant but a first-time hearer may not immediately parse it as a place/directory name | Yes, generic | 5 letters |
| **Chauraha** | Medium — three syllables, "-raha" ending needs care | Medium-high — vivid image (crossroads) | Yes, generic | 8 letters, longest on the list |
| **Basera** | Easy | Medium — "nest/home" reads more like a real-estate or housing brand than a business directory | Yes, generic | 6 letters |

Ten candidates carried forward to availability research: **Aaspaas, Karib, Nukkad, Padosi, Mohalla, Nazdeek, Sang, Milke, Chauraha, Basera.**

**Bottom line on availability:** almost every common, obviously-fitting Hindi-Urdu word for "nearby" or "neighborhood" is already in active use by an Indian (or regional) startup — this is a genuinely crowded naming space, not a failure of brainstorming. **Karib** is the one candidate in the shortlist with no material collision found. **Sang** and **Milke** are also clear but carry more of the "explain what this word means" burden in early marketing.

## 4. Phrase test

Testing how each of the three cleared/near-clear names sits inside real usage sentences:

| Phrase | Karib | Sang | Milke |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Find it on ___" | "Find it on Karib." — clean, reads as a place name. | "Find it on Sang." — works, slightly flatter. | "Find it on Milke." — works, faint "typo?" hesitation on first read. |
| "Ask ___" | "Ask Karib." — reads naturally, almost like asking a knowledgeable local person named Karib. | "Ask Sang." — reads like a person's name too; fine. | "Ask Milke." — a little awkward, "Milke" as a name is less natural. |
| "Listed on ___" | "Listed on Karib." — clean. | "Listed on Sang." — clean. | "Listed on Milke." — clean. |
| "Chat with a local through ___" | "Chat with a local through Karib." — clean, and "Karib" reinforcing closeness actually strengthens this sentence. | "Chat with a local through Sang." — clean but "Sang" (with/together) is a slightly odd fit for "through." | "Chat with a local through Milke." — clean. |

**Karib wins the phrase test outright** — it's the only one of the three where the *meaning* of the word (closeness) reinforces the sentence it sits in, not just its sound.

## 5. Recommendation

### Primary: **Karib**

- No material naming collision found in India's local-discovery/directory space (the riskiest and most obvious names — Aaspaas, Nukkad, Padosi, Mohalla, Nazdeek, Chauraha — are all taken by real, sometimes well-funded competitors in adjacent categories).
- Means "near/close" — directly encodes the product's core promise (reachable, nearby) without having to explain it.
- Short (5 letters), fast to type one-handed on WhatsApp, no ambiguous spelling.
- Generalizes cleanly to a second city — nothing about it is Lucknow-specific or even UP-specific; it's shared Hindi-Urdu vocabulary understood pan-India.
- Passes the phrase test better than any other candidate — "Ask Karib," in particular, personifies the product as a helpful, present local guide, which matches the "neighborly" voice principle directly.
- Trade-off to flag honestly: it's a less *instantly evocative* word than Nukkad or Aaspaas for a Hindi-speaking ear — it will do less "explaining itself" on first hearing than a word like Nukkad would if that name weren't taken. The tagline needs to carry some of that weight (see `02-positioning-and-messaging.md`).

### Backup 1: **Sang**

- Cleanest legal/availability profile of any candidate on the list.
- Extremely WhatsApp-friendly (4 letters).
- Weaker on its own — "with/together" doesn't say "nearby" or "place" the way Karib does, so brand education costs more upfront.

### Backup 2: **Milke**

- Also clean, warm, and invites action ("let's meet") rather than just describing proximity — a nice emotional register for a resident-first product.
- Weakest on immediate legibility as a place/product name — reads more like a verb or greeting than a noun, and needs more supporting copy to land as a brand name.

**Do not shortlist Aaspaas, Nukkad, Padosi, Mohalla, Nazdeek, or Chauraha as finalists** without a founder-level decision to accept real collision risk — several of these are used by funded competitors in the same or an adjacent category, which is a materially different risk than "a similar-sounding word exists somewhere."

## 6. Next steps before locking a name

1. Run the top 2–3 candidates through IP India's public trademark search (free, self-serve) — this document is not that check.
2. Confirm `.in` and `.com` availability directly through a registrar (Aaspaas's own domain history above shows how easily search-based guesses can be wrong).
3. Check exact-match availability on Instagram, WhatsApp Business display name, and Google Play/App Store listing names (a taken app-store name is a harder blocker than a taken domain).
4. Say the finalist out loud to 5–10 real Lucknow residents outside the project — the single best test this document cannot substitute for.
