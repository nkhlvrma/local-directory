# Business Outreach Scripts

Fuller conversational scripts for a founder visiting or calling a business in person to invite them into the directory — not just message templates, but the actual back-and-forth, including objection handling. Written for **[Site Name]** (Karib is only the current recommendation) in [City] (Lucknow).

---

## In-person visit script

### Opening (first 10 seconds — the only part that matters for whether they keep listening)

> "Namaste! Do minute ka time hai? Main [City] mein ek chhoti si directory bana raha hoon local shops/services ki — bilkul free hai, aapko kuch nahi karna, bas ek WhatsApp number confirm karna hai."

*(Namaste! Got two minutes? I'm building a small directory of local shops and services in [City] — completely free, you don't have to do anything except confirm a WhatsApp number.)*

Say this standing, not sitting, and keep it under 15 seconds. The goal of the opening is only to earn the next 60 seconds, not to explain the whole product.

### The pitch (once they're listening)

> "Simple hai — log [Site Name] pe [category] search karte hain, aapki listing dikhti hai, aur wo seedha aapke WhatsApp pe message karte hain. Koi app download nahi, koi form nahi, koi fees nahi. Bas aapka number verify karna hai — ek message bhejunga abhi, aap reply kar dena, done."

*(It's simple — people search for [category] on [Site Name], your listing shows up, and they message you directly on your WhatsApp. No app to download, no form, no fees. I just need to verify your number — I'll send a message right now, you reply, and we're done.)*

### Live verification (do this in the same visit, not later)

> "Ye lijiye, abhi ek WhatsApp message bhej raha hoon aapke number pe — dekh lijiye aa raha hai."

*(Here, sending a WhatsApp message to your number right now — check that it's coming through.)*

Send the actual outreach/verification message live, on the spot, and watch it deliver. This does two things: it proves the mechanic instantly (no trust-me-later gap), and it collapses what would otherwise be a separate follow-up step into the same visit — a meaningfully higher completion rate than "I'll message you in a day or two."

### Close

> "Aapki permission mil gayi — main [Site Name] pe listing review ke liye daal raha hoon. Live hone ke baad link bhej dunga. Agar kabhi hatana ho ya kuch change karna ho, bas mujhe message kar dena."

*(Thanks — with your permission, I'll submit the listing for review and send you the live link. If you ever want to be removed or change something, just message me.)*

## Phone call script (when an in-person visit isn't practical)

### Opening

> "Namaste, [Name] bol rahe hain [Business]? Main [founder name] hoon, [City] mein ek local directory bana raha hoon, [Site Name] naam se. Do minute mil sakte hain?"

*(Hello, is this [Name] from [Business]? I'm [founder name], building a local directory in [City] called [Site Name]. Do you have two minutes?)*

### Pitch and verification
Same core pitch as the in-person version, but since you can't watch them receive the message live: 

> "Main abhi aapke is number pe ek WhatsApp message bhejta hoon — aap bas ek baar reply kar dena, jaise 'haan' ya 'ok', bas itna hi. Uske baad aap live ho jaayenge."

*(I'll send a WhatsApp message to this number right now — just reply once, even "yes" or "ok", if you consent to the listing. I'll send the live link after review.)*

Stay on the call (or promise to check back within a few minutes) until the reply comes through, rather than treating it as a separate follow-up task — the drop-off between "said yes on the phone" and "actually confirmed later" is the single biggest place this funnel leaks.

## Objection handling

### Objection 1: "Why would I pay / give you my number for free?"

This objection usually conflates two different concerns — "is this a scam trying to get my number" and "what's the catch if it's free" — so answer both, briefly:

> "Bilkul free hai, koi payment nahi maang raha hoon — na abhi, na baad mein kabhi bhi bina bataye. Aapka number sirf isliye chahiye taaki log seedha aapse WhatsApp pe baat kar saken, jaise wo already kar rahe honge apne doston se. Main sirf ek message bhej ke confirm kar raha hoon ki number sahi hai, taaki jo bhi listing dekhe wo bharosa kar sake ki reply milega."

*(It's completely free, I'm not asking for payment — not now, and never later without telling you first. I need your number only so people can message you directly on WhatsApp, the same way they already message their own friends. I'm just sending one message to confirm the number is correct, so anyone who sees the listing can trust they'll get a reply.)*

If pressed further on "what's in it for you": be honest, don't invent a bigger mission than exists.

> "Abhi ke liye main sirf ek achhi, kaam ki directory banana chahta hoon [City] ke liye. Aage jaake shायद kuch business tools bhi banaunga jinke liye paisa lagega, lekin wo alag se hoga aur aapko bataunga pehle — ye listing hamesha free rahegi."

*(For now I just want to build a genuinely useful directory for [City]. Later I might build some paid business tools, but that would be separate and I'd tell you first — this listing stays free.)*

### Objection 2: "I'm already on Google / Justdial, why do I need this too?"

Don't argue that Karib replaces Google or Justdial — that's both untrue and unnecessary to claim.

> "Google aur Justdial pe toh already ho, wo theek hai — [Site Name] alag cheez hai. Yahan log seedha aapke WhatsApp pe message karte hain, call nahi karte, form nahi bharte. Aur Justdial pe toh bahut saare purane, band ho chuke numbers pade hote hain — yahan main khud check karta hoon ki number chal raha hai, taaki jo bhi contact kare use bharosa ho."

*(You're already on Google and Justdial, that's fine — [Site Name] is a different thing. Here people message you directly on WhatsApp, not calls, not forms. And on Justdial there are a lot of old, dead numbers listed — here I personally check the number works, so anyone who contacts you can trust it.)*

If they still seem unconvinced, make the ask smaller rather than pushing harder:

> "Koi zabardasti nahi — bas ek WhatsApp reply ka kaam hai, do minute ka. Agar useful na lage toh kabhi bhi hata sakte hain."

*(No pressure — it's just one WhatsApp reply, two minutes. If it doesn't turn out useful you can be removed anytime.)*

### Objection 3: "I don't have time for this right now / come back later"

The instinct is to push for the sale now — resist it. A rushed "yes" from someone who didn't actually engage often doesn't convert to a real reply on the verification message.

> "Bilkul, koi jaldi nahi. Main [day/time] ko wapas aa jaata hoon, ya aap jab free ho tab mujhe ek WhatsApp kar dena, main turant listing kar dunga."

*(No problem, no rush. I'll come back on [day/time], or whenever you're free just WhatsApp me and I'll list you right away.)*

Leave a physical trace they can act on later without the founder present — a small card or note with the WhatsApp number to message, since a verbal "come back later" without a way to reconnect usually just doesn't happen.

### Objection 4 (bonus — commonly comes up): "How do I know this isn't a scam / fake listing service?"

> "Samajh sakta hoon ye sawaal. Dekhiye, main koi payment nahi maang raha, na koi personal detail jaise Aadhaar ya bank account. Aapki permission ke bina number publish nahi hoga, aur aap kabhi bhi removal bol sakte hain. Aap [site URL] pe khud dekh sakte hain — yahan contact checked ka matlab sirf itna hai ki number par reply mila, business endorsement nahi."

*(I understand that concern. I'm not asking for payment or personal details like Aadhaar or a bank account. You can decide whether you want the number published, and you can ask for removal later. You can also check [site URL] yourself — the site only claims that a contact was checked, not that every business is endorsed.)*

Having a real, checkable site with real listings to point to is the single best answer to this objection — which is also why Phase 1 of the acquisition plan (`07-acquisition-playbooks.md`) sequences supply-building before any public-facing content: there needs to be something real to point to before this objection comes up at scale.

## General notes for whoever runs these scripts

- These are scripts to internalize and adapt in the moment, not to read verbatim off a phone — a business owner can tell when someone's reading a script, and it undercuts the whole "neighborly, not corporate" positioning.
- Hinglish is written as the default register here because it's the natural spoken register for this exact interaction (per the tone notes recovered from `src/lib/outreach.ts`) — switch to more English or more Hindi based on how the specific person is speaking, not by a fixed rule.
- If an objection comes up that isn't covered here, the fallback rule is the same as the four scripts above: answer honestly and specifically, make the immediate ask as small as possible, and never claim something (revenue plans, quality guarantees, scale) that isn't true yet.
