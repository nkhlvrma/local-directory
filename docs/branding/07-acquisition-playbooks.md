# Acquisition Playbooks — 90-Day Plan, Made Actionable

Turns the three phases (days 1–30 supply, 31–60 resident usage, 61–90 repeatability) into checklists specific enough to act on directly. Written for a solo, low-budget founder — every task assumes no team, no ad budget, and channels the founder already has access to (WhatsApp, personal network, local community groups).

---

## Phase 1 (Days 1–30): Supply — get real, reachable listings on the directory

**Goal for the phase:** a critical mass of verified listings in 2–3 categories a resident would actually search for in week one, concentrated in 2–3 neighborhoods rather than spread thin citywide.

### Week-by-week checklist

**Days 1–7 — Pick the beachhead**
- [ ] Choose 2–3 starting categories based on what's genuinely easy to verify and recruit fast (electricians, tiffin/food delivery, tutors, salons are typically dense and WhatsApp-native in tier-2 Indian cities — but confirm against what's actually common in the founder's own Lucknow neighborhoods rather than assuming).
- [ ] Choose 1–2 starting neighborhoods — ideally ones the founder already has some personal or family connection to, since the first 20–30 listings will mostly come from walking around and asking, not cold outreach.
- [ ] Build a simple running list (spreadsheet is fine) of every business in scope: name, category, phone number, whether it has a real WhatsApp presence already (many small businesses use a personal number for WhatsApp — check before assuming a landline-only business is out of scope).
- [ ] Draft and finalize the outreach message templates (see `09-whatsapp-templates.md`) before making first contact — don't improvise message-by-message.

**Days 8–14 — First outreach wave**
- [ ] Visit or call 15–20 businesses in person from the list (in-person or phone first, WhatsApp message second — a cold WhatsApp message from an unknown number to a shop owner who's never heard of Karib has a much lower reply rate than a message that follows a face-to-face or phone introduction). See `10-business-outreach-scripts.md` for the actual conversation script.
- [ ] For each business that says yes: get their WhatsApp number confirmed live, in front of them if possible (send a test message and watch it deliver) — this collapses the "we'll message you in a day or two" verification step into the same visit, which is both faster and more reliable than doing it remotely later.
- [ ] Track a simple conversion funnel even informally: businesses approached → said yes → number confirmed live → listing published. This is the seed data for the measurement plan (`11-measurement-plan.md`).
- [ ] Target for the week: **10 live, verified listings.** Adjust down if the actual in-person conversion rate is lower than expected — the point of week 2 is calibrating a realistic per-visit yield, not hitting an arbitrary number.

**Days 15–21 — Recruit neighborhood connectors**
A "neighborhood connector" is a real person embedded in a neighborhood — a local RWA (resident welfare association) member, a well-known shopkeeper, a building WhatsApp group admin — who can vouch for Karib to other businesses and residents, dramatically lowering the cold-outreach cost per listing.
- [ ] Identify 3–5 candidate connectors per starting neighborhood: RWA/society WhatsApp group admins, a well-liked shop owner who already knows everyone else on the street, a local parent group organizer.
- [ ] Approach each candidate directly and personally (not via a generic message) — the ask is specific: "Can I add you as someone I can call if I want an intro to a shop owner here?" This is a much smaller, lower-commitment ask than "be an ambassador," and should be framed that way.
- [ ] Offer something concrete and non-monetary in return where possible in the early phase — e.g., being the first person told about new listings in their area, or a personal thank-you — rather than a cash incentive Karib can't sustain at this stage.
- [ ] Use each connector's introduction to reach 5–10 more businesses this week with a warmer opening (see the "warm intro" variant script in `10-business-outreach-scripts.md`).

**Days 22–30 — Consolidate and fill gaps**
- [ ] Review category and neighborhood coverage: are there obvious gaps a resident would notice immediately (e.g. no tutors listed at all, or all listings clustered in one street)? Prioritize filling those over adding more of an already-covered category.
- [ ] Re-contact any business that said "maybe later" in week 1–2 — a second, warmer touch (especially if a connector can now vouch) converts meaningfully better than a single cold attempt.
- [ ] Target for phase end: **40–60 live, verified listings** across the chosen categories/neighborhoods. This is a planning anchor, not a guarantee — the real target should be set from what weeks 1–2 show is achievable per week, then extrapolated.
- [ ] Draft the "verified local businesses" WhatsApp share card (see below) so it's ready to go the moment Phase 2 starts — don't build this under time pressure once residents are already the focus.

## Phase 2 (Days 31–60): Resident usage — get real people searching and messaging

**Goal for the phase:** residents in the launch neighborhoods know Karib exists, search it when they actually need a local service, and enough of them click through to WhatsApp that the core loop (search → view → WhatsApp) is proven.

### Week-by-week checklist

**Days 31–37 — Soft launch to the founder's own network**
- [ ] Share the site personally (not as a broadcast) with people in the founder's own building/society/neighborhood WhatsApp groups — a personal message from a known person ("hey, I built this, would love your feedback") converts and gets honest feedback far better than a cold link drop.
- [ ] Ask a specific small number of people (10–15) to actually try one real search for something they need and report back what happened — this is the first real signal on whether search + WhatsApp click actually works end to end for a stranger to the product.
- [ ] Fix anything broken or confusing that this reveals before pushing to a wider audience — a bad first impression in a tight-knit community WhatsApp group spreads exactly as fast as a good one.

**Days 38–44 — Seed practical questions in local groups**
- [ ] Post 3–4 genuinely useful, non-promotional questions/answers in local community WhatsApp groups and neighborhood Facebook groups (see draft examples below) — the goal is to be helpful first, mention Karib naturally second, not the reverse.
- [ ] Post the "verified local businesses" share card (see below) once, in 2–3 relevant groups, framed as a genuinely useful resource, not an announcement about a company.
- [ ] Start a WhatsApp Status habit (personal number, or a Karib business number if one exists) — 2–3 times a week, short and specific ("Found a great tiffin service in [neighborhood], verified their WhatsApp, added them — search 'tiffin' on Karib"), not generic promotion.

**Days 45–52 — Instagram presence, lightweight**
- [ ] Set up a basic Instagram account if not already done — even a simple, consistent one. Post 2–3 times this week: one listing spotlight, one "did you know Karib checks WhatsApp numbers" trust-building post, one neighborhood-specific post (e.g. "Best of [neighborhood] on Karib so far").
- [ ] Cross-post WhatsApp Status content to Instagram Stories where it makes sense — don't create two entirely separate content streams for one founder to maintain.

**Days 53–60 — Push on the weakest funnel step**
- [ ] By this point there should be enough usage data (see `11-measurement-plan.md`) to see where residents drop off — most likely candidates: they land on the homepage but don't search, or they view a listing but don't click WhatsApp. Whichever it is, spend this week specifically addressing that step (copy, layout, or trust-signal changes) rather than continuing to add top-of-funnel content blindly.
- [ ] Ask 5–10 residents who used the site directly what almost stopped them from messaging a business — qualitative signal a dashboard alone won't give.

## Phase 3 (Days 61–90): Repeatability — prove the playbook works without heroic one-off effort

**Goal for the phase:** demonstrate that supply recruitment and resident acquisition can run as a repeatable weekly cadence, not a one-time launch push, and identify what parts of the first 60 days genuinely need the founder personally vs. what can become a lighter-touch, semi-systematic process.

### Week-by-week checklist

**Days 61–67 — Document what actually worked**
- [ ] Write down, honestly, which supply-recruitment channel produced the most listings per hour of founder time: in-person visits, connector introductions, or something else that emerged. Same for resident acquisition: which channel (WhatsApp groups, Instagram, Status) produced the most real usage, not just impressions.
- [ ] Kill or deprioritize whatever produced the least — a solo founder's time is the actual scarce resource here, not ideas for new channels.

**Days 68–74 — Systemize the winning supply channel**
- [ ] If connector-driven recruitment worked best: formalize the connector relationship slightly (a short, clear message template for what to send a connector monthly, a simple way to track which connector referred which business) so it doesn't rely on the founder remembering informally.
- [ ] If in-person visits worked best: block a recurring weekly time (e.g. every Tuesday afternoon) for a fixed number of new business visits, treating it as a standing commitment rather than an ad hoc task.

**Days 75–81 — Expand within existing neighborhoods before going wider**
- [ ] Add 1–2 new categories to the existing launch neighborhoods (deepen, don't yet widen) — this tests whether the recruitment playbook generalizes to a new category before testing whether it generalizes to a new area.
- [ ] Re-run the "verified local businesses" share card push in the same groups, now with a noticeably larger, more credible listing count — the second push in the same community should land better than the first because there's now visible proof it worked.

**Days 82–90 — Decide readiness for a second neighborhood/city**
- [ ] Pick one new neighborhood in Lucknow (not yet a second city) and run the full supply playbook there in compressed form (one week instead of four) as a rehearsal — this is the most honest test of repeatability available before committing to the bigger step of a second city.
- [ ] Use the outcome of that rehearsal, plus 90 days of real funnel data, to fill out `12-second-city-checklist.md` — don't decide on a second city from the original plan's assumptions alone; decide from what days 1–90 actually showed.

## Neighborhood connectors — recruitment approach

Who to target, concretely:
- RWA (Resident Welfare Association) committee members — they already have authority and trust within a residential area and often already run a building/society WhatsApp group.
- The most-followed local shop owner on a given street — often a kirana (grocery) store owner or a well-established business that's been there for years; other shopkeepers already trust and listen to them.
- Admins of hyperlocal Facebook groups or WhatsApp groups (parenting groups, society groups, "Lucknow locals" style groups) — they control distribution, not just individual trust.

How to approach them: in person or by phone first, never a cold broadcast message. The ask should be small and specific ("Can I ask you a couple of questions about shops in this area, and maybe call you if I need an intro?"), not "will you be our ambassador," which is a bigger, vaguer commitment that's easy to decline.

## "Verified local businesses" WhatsApp share card — draft copy

Format: a short forwardable text message (not an image, at this stage — a plain-text message is more likely to actually get forwarded person-to-person than an image card a founder would need design tooling to produce; revisit as an image once there's bandwidth).

**English:**
> Found this useful — a small directory of local [neighborhood] businesses that actually reply on WhatsApp. No forms, just tap and message them directly: [link]
> Might be handy if you're looking for [category example, e.g. an electrician or tiffin service] nearby.

**Hinglish:**
> Ye kaam ki cheez mili — [neighborhood] ke local businesses ki ek chhoti directory, jo WhatsApp pe reply karte hain. Koi form nahi, bas tap karke seedha message karo: [link]
> Agar aas-paas [category example] chahiye ho toh kaam aa sakta hai.

**Hindi:**
> ये काम की चीज़ मिली — [neighborhood] के local businesses की एक छोटी directory, जो WhatsApp पर reply करते हैं। कोई form नहीं, बस tap करके सीधा message करो: [link]
> अगर आस-पास [category example] चाहिए हो तो काम आ सकता है।

## Seeding practical questions in local groups — 4 example posts

The rule: be genuinely useful first. Karib should be mentioned as an answer, not as the subject of the post.

1. **"Does anyone know a reliable AC repair guy in [neighborhood] who actually picks up the phone? Tried two numbers from an old flyer and both are dead."**
   *(Wait for replies, then, if genuinely helpful:)* "Found one on Karib actually — messaged them on WhatsApp directly and they replied in a few minutes. [link]"

2. **"PSA for anyone new to [neighborhood] — tiffin services here are mostly WhatsApp-only, no Swiggy/Zomato listing. Compiled a few I found reachable if useful: [link]"**

3. **"Looking for a tutor for 8th grade Maths near [neighborhood], preferably someone who'll actually respond and not ghost after one message 😅 Any recommendations?"**
   *(Genuine ask — if the founder's own directory can answer it, follow up naturally once replies come in, don't lead with the product.)*

4. **"Small thing I've been doing — before recommending a local shop/service to someone, I try messaging their number on WhatsApp first to make sure it's not dead. Built a tiny list of ones that check out if anyone wants it: [link]"**
   This one is the most directly "founder explains what they built," but frames it as a personal habit/project rather than a company announcement — still needs to be used sparingly (once per group, not repeated) to avoid reading as promotional.

**General rule across all four:** never post the same exact wording in multiple groups back-to-back on the same day — it reads as copy-pasted spam even if well-intentioned. Vary phrasing, and space posts across different groups over different days.
